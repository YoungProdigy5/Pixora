How to set a wallpaper on your phone

Download the image from PIXORA first. Menu names differ between phone brands and software versions, so use these steps as a guide.

Android

  • Open the downloaded image in your Gallery or Photos app.
  • Tap the three-dot menu and choose Set as wallpaper (sometimes Use as).
  • Choose Home screen, Lock screen, or both, then confirm.
  • Another way: press and hold an empty space on the home screen, tap Wallpaper & style, and pick the image from your photos.

iPhone

  • Open the image in the Photos app and tap the Share button.
  • Choose Use as Wallpaper, then adjust the position.
  • Tap Done and choose whether to set it for both screens or customise the Home screen separately.
  • Another way: Settings > Wallpaper > Add New Wallpaper > Photos.

Choosing the right size

A wallpaper looks sharpest when its pixel size is at least as large as your screen. To find your screen resolution, search your phone model online, or check the display or "About phone" settings.

  • Most modern phones are tall, narrow screens, so choose a portrait (upright) image.
  • If the image is smaller than your screen, the phone enlarges it and it can look soft or blurry.
  • Some Android home screens scroll sideways, so an image slightly wider than the screen can look better.
  • Keep the main subject (a face, a car, a logo) away from the edges and away from where the clock sits on the lock screen.

Do dark and AMOLED wallpapers save battery?

On OLED and AMOLED screens, each pixel makes its own light, and a truly black pixel is switched off. A mostly black wallpaper therefore uses a little less power than a bright one on those screens.

  • The saving is usually small, and it depends on how long your screen is on and how bright it is.
  • On older LCD screens the backlight stays on, so a dark wallpaper does not save battery.
  • Dark wallpapers also look good with dark mode, and they can be easier on the eyes at night.

Not sure which screen you have? Search your phone model's specifications and look for "AMOLED" or "OLED".
